Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about hair transplantation

What happens during the consultation?
During your free consultation, we'll assess your hair loss, discuss your goals, and create a personalized treatment plan. We'll explain everything in detail and answer all your questions. There's no pressure or obligation!
How long does the procedure take?
It varies depending on the number of grafts being transplanted, but most procedures take 4-8 hours. Larger cases may take longer, or we may split them into multiple sessions. During consultation, we'll give you a precise time estimate.
Is the procedure painful?
No, the procedure is performed under local anesthesia so you won't feel any pain during the surgery. You may feel some pressure or discomfort during the anesthesia administration, but once it takes effect, the procedure is pain-free.
What is the post-operative care like?
We'll provide you with detailed aftercare instructions. This typically includes keeping the area clean, avoiding strenuous activity for a few days, and following specific washing instructions. Most patients return to desk work within 2-3 days.
When can I wash my hair after the procedure?
We'll show you exactly how to wash your hair carefully starting from the day after your procedure. We use a gentle, specific washing technique to protect the transplanted follicles while keeping the area clean.
Will I need follow-up appointments?
Yes! We schedule follow-up appointments to check your healing and progress. For international patients, we can also do virtual follow-ups. We're committed to your success and will support you every step of the way!
What should I bring to my consultation?
Just bring yourself! It's helpful to have photos of your hair from earlier years if you have them. If you've tried any hair loss treatments before, we'd love to know about them. That's it!
Can I drive myself home after the procedure?
No, we recommend having someone drive you home or arranging transportation. While the procedure is pain-free, you'll be given medications that can make driving unsafe.
When can I exercise again?
We recommend avoiding strenuous exercise for the first 7-10 days. Light walking is fine, but anything that makes you sweat or raises your blood pressure should wait. We'll give you a clear timeline for resuming all activities.
Is there anything I should avoid before surgery?
Yes! We'll give you a full list, but generally avoid blood thinners (including aspirin and some supplements), alcohol, and smoking in the days leading up to your procedure.
